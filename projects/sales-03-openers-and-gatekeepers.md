# Openers and gatekeepers

Method page. The business, the owner and the examples are made up for this page. The rules are mine. This is not a results report. See [why these projects use made-up data](../CONFIDENTIALITY.md).

## What I do: answer the pushback before it comes up
Most calls end because the person has an easy way out in the first ten seconds. So the opener is built to take those exits away before they are used. Here is the structure, on a made-up owner:

"Hey Dave, I have a few minutes before I have to handle something really important, but I needed to get this off my desk before I send you that email. I'm not even sure if this is for you, but you could just walk me through it and we'll figure it out."

![An opener broken into its parts](../assets/sales-s5-opener-anatomy.png)
Recreated illustration. Marlow Pest Control is made up.

What each part does:
1. **I leave the call first.** I tell him I have a few minutes, so I am the one who will end the call. He does not need to reach for the exit before I do.
2. **The email is already coming.** I say I am about to send him that email, so "send me an email" is not available as a way out. The email is already on its way.
3. **I am not sure it is for him.** I have not pitched him and I have not qualified him, so "not interested" has nothing to push against. I am asking him to confirm something for me, not to buy something.

Then comes one question from my research, a poke the bear question about something real. See [research before the call](sales-02-research-before-the-call.md). When a client gives me a script, I keep to it and shape the first line inside it.

## More rules for an opener
1. **Name first.** I start with the owner's real name, found in my research, so the first thing they hear is their own name.
2. **No company name up front.** An opener that starts with "I'm with company X" is burned. The owner has heard it dozens of times and stops listening at the company name.
3. **Name the specific pain.** I say what customers are saying, not where I found it. "A few of your customers say they left a voicemail and nobody called back" sounds like something I know. "I was reading your Google reviews" sounds like a script.
4. **Say we, not they.** When I describe what we do, I say we. I do not create distance between me and the work.

## Gatekeepers
I open as if the owner picked up. I use the owner's researched name and the specific thing I am calling about, in the tone of an ordinary call. I do not ask permission to speak to anyone, I do not negotiate, and I do not accept the first no. A receptionist's no is a routine filter. It is not a decision the owner made.
